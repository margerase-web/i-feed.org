Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Policy in Korea, Japan, China, the United Kingdom, the United States, France and Germany, 2020

This report reviews the entrepreneurial ecosystem policy that the governments of Korea, Japan, China, the United Kingdom, the United States, France and Germany set out from January to December 2020 in six domains — regulation and law, finance, culture, support, human capital and markets — and analyses it in its context, against theory and in practice. Pandemic relief took most of the money and reached firms by their past payroll, turnover or income, so that new founders were reached only where the rules made room for them. The strongest measures were those that put public money beside private investors, such as Korea’s Fund of Funds, the British Future Fund, France’s bridge fund and Germany’s matching facility, and the insolvency rules in force in the United Kingdom, the United States and France; the weakest were the aims, awards and proclamations offered for culture and the British cut in the relief on founders’ exits. Many reforms were left as bills or passed without promulgation shown, and performance was reported as outputs, not effects.
